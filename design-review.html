Design review · RMIS v4.2

Strong system, four leaks in the foundation.

A surface-by-surface audit of the Recruitment Management Information System — 112 components, 13 signed-in and public views, one design language. The visual voice is coherent and unusually disciplined for an in-house government system. What follows is not a redesign: it is a list of places where the implementation has quietly drifted from its own spec, and what each drift costs the people using it.

3.3

The system is 80% there and the remaining 20% is concentrated. Nearly every issue below traces to the same root cause: a design spec that was written carefully, then implemented call-site by call-site, so the spec lives in the CSS comments rather than in the tokens. Scores above describe the code as reviewed; the four P0/P1 items have since been implemented and verified in a browser — see What shipped.

P0

The primary blue fails AA every time it carries text

RMIS Electric Blue #1591dc is the app's single chromatic voice, and it is used in three incompatible roles at once: a fill behind white text, a text colour on white, and a non-text indicator. The first two fail WCAG AA.

Evidence

The fix is not a rebrand. Split the single hex into two named roles of the same hue. #0b6fa8 already exists in the codebase four times — someone reached for it informally. Formalise it.

Proposed role split

brand-600#0b6fa8 · text + fill
brand-700#0a6295 · hover / pressed

brand-500 stays everywhere colour is a graphic: focus rings, the 3px rail indicator, chart series 1, stage dots, illustration, display type at 24px+. brand-600 takes every role where the colour must be read: button fills, links, inline emphasis. At a glance the two are the same blue; at a screen reader or a projector they are not.

The 10px tier is the urgent part. It is used for the range picker's month labels (motion/date-range-picker.tsx:184), small badge text (reui/badge.tsx:32), audit row stamps (ui/audit-row.tsx:43) and — worst — the footer's Data Privacy Act notice (shell/footer.tsx:53), which is legal text at 10px and 4.28:1 on the fog surface.

Two smaller leaks in the same area

  • Page titles are below the scale. PageHeader renders text-2xl / sm:text-[28px] (ui/shell.tsx:95), while the token scale reserves 33px for a "card heading". The page title is therefore smaller than a heading is supposed to be — the ladder is inverted at the top.
  • 51 eyebrows. The caps micro-label appears 51 times. When nearly every block opens with a label, the label stops signalling "this is a category" and becomes decoration. Reserve it for stat ledgers, rail groups, and blocks that open with a number.
P1

Three radius names, one value — so no control reads as a control

The theme maps --radius-lg, --radius-xl and --radius-2xl all to 16px (globals.css:355–362). Call sites still choose between them as if they were different steps: rounded-lg 72×, rounded-xl 45×, rounded-2xl 26×, rounded-3xl 33×, rounded-full 140× — 332 declarations, three visual values.

The practical cost is not tidiness. A 44px input with a 16px radius is 73% rounded — it reads as a soft pill, nearly identical to the 12px buttons and the 9999px filter chips beside it. When a card, a field, a button and a chip all wear the same silhouette, the interface loses the "what can I press" signal and users scan more slowly.

P1

Two button systems, and the smaller target is on the touchscreen

ui/buttons.ts is the declared single source of truth: 12px radius, 44px height, no shadow. ui/button.tsx is a shadcn cva variant that survived the migration: fully rounded, 36px default, drop shadow, hover lift — three things the design spec explicitly forbids. It is still imported by two files.

Two "primary" buttons, one bundleSame action weight

button.tsx cva · 36px · pill · shadow

Rounded-full, 36px, casts a shadow and lifts 1px on hover.

buttons.ts ctaBtn · 44px · 12px · flat

12px radius, 44px, flat. This is what 26 other files use.

The inverted breakpoint

sectionCtaBtn (ui/buttons.ts:71) is h-9 min-h-[36px] on phones and sm:h-10 sm:min-h-[44px] from 640px up. The comment explains the intent — keep desktop-sized buttons off phones — but the effect is the opposite of what touch needs: 36px where fingers are, 44px where a mouse is.

Across the app: 19 controls at min-h-[36px] and 19 square icon buttons at h-9 w-9. That is roughly one in six interactive elements below the 44px target — concentrated in exactly the places people use on a phone: table rows, section headers, row actions.

Compactness is a visual property, not a hit-area property. Keep the 36px look on phones by tightening horizontal padding and dropping to 13px text, but hold the height at 44px. If a row genuinely cannot afford 44px, keep the 36px box and extend the hit area with a pseudo-element, as the rail already does with min-h-[44px].
P1

Every view shouts at the same volume

The review queue is the clearest example, and it is a one-line problem. Its page header is a 24px title, a one-line description, a neutral count pill, a view-mode segmented control and a refresh button — all quiet and correct. Then the stage filters arrive: five capsules in which the selected one is a solid brand-blue pill with a drop shadow (shadow-cta), the same treatment the app uses for "Send interview invitations".

So the most button-like element in the header is a filter. On a page whose real commitments are row-level actions, the brand fill is being spent on a lens — and the eye reads six things of equal weight before it finds anything to do.

Rule to adopt

  • One solid fill per view. The view's most consequential action owns the brand fill; everything else is ghost, text, or hairline chip. Shipped: the review queue's row actions are now the only blue fills on the page — which is why the table reads as "these are the things you do".
  • Filters never wear the brand fill. Selected state is ink (bg-foreground text-background), which also inverts correctly in dark mode. Shipped in FilterChip.
  • Eyebrows must introduce a number or a category. Not a sentence. 51 call sites is 40 too many — left as editorial work.
  • Page title at 24, section title at 18, card title at 18. Three levels, then stop — the body is 16, so a fourth level would be invisible. Shipped: PageHeader is now the 24px display step at every width (it used to grow to 28 and outrank the "card heading" token).

P2

Staff navigation on a phone is seven tabs held together by pixel arithmetic

An admin's mobile bar carries Dashboard · Jobs · Users · Review · Analytics · Settings · Account — seven targets in a 390px dock, about 48px each before padding. To make that fit, shell/mobile-tab-bar.tsx contains:

  • a label-shortening map ("Review Queue" → "Review");
  • a hidden-span measurement hook with a ResizeObserver, mirroring the vendored block's own measurement;
  • a shell-width formula (rowShellWidth) plus per-tab zero-height spacers so the open and closed bars match to the pixel;
  • a positional CSS mirror (MIRROR_BAR_CLASS, keyed by tab count 4/6/7) that reverses icon and label order on the right half of the bar via nth-child selectors and flex-row-reverse;
  • a !important padding override to shave the sizer's dock reserve back to 54px.

That is roughly 150 lines of geometry engineered to avoid an overflow menu — and each of those mechanisms is a future bug when a role gains a section.

P2

Two greens mean two different things, and one of them can't hold text

The palette carries five status hues, five data-viz series, three category accents and two washes — 15 chromatic tokens for a system whose own spec says "one chromatic voice". Two of them are green, and they mean different things:

TokenMeaningWhite text on it
1--ok #1f7a3dSuccess / shortlisted5.37 — safe
2--color-emerald-accent #34c771Category coding2.20 — unreadable
3--viz-2 #31ce01Chart series 22.11 — unreadable

Nothing currently places white text on the last two — but they sit in the same token block as tokens that do carry text, and the only thing preventing the mistake is memory.

One credit before the criticism: StageDot is not colour-only. It renders the same five-glyph set inside a 20px tinted disc, so a filter chip still speaks in shape — better than most systems manage. The inconsistency is smaller than it first looks: the same state is drawn at two different scales (a 12px inline glyph in a pill, a 20px disc in a chip), so a stage reads slightly differently depending on where you meet it.

Proposal

  • One green family. Delete --color-emerald-accent and --viz-2; make chart series 2 a tint of --ok. Category coding can borrow from the existing coral / cobalt / lilac set.
  • Category accents are tints, never fills behind text. #34c771 at 12% behind ink text is fine; #34c771 as a chip background with white text never is.
  • One scale per context for status. Keep the 5-glyph set everywhere; make the filter chip use the pill's 12px inline glyph rather than a 20px disc, so a stage reads identically in the header and in the table.
  • Retire the .dark .bg-white sweep (globals.css:1033–1050) and the 46 hardcoded hexes in TSX. Dark mode currently works by patching call sites, which means every new component is a new dark-mode bug.

Shipped

The three greens collapsed onto one value, #2f9e57 — --viz-2, --chart-2, --color-recovery-green and --color-emerald-accent now all point at it. It measures 3.42:1 on white (the old #31ce01 was 2.11, below the 3:1 that any chart mark owes its canvas), it stays in the same family as --ok, and it remains clearly distinct from the gold, lilac and coral series beside it — verified on the Analytics funnel and the status donut.

In the running app

Deliberately not done — and why

  • Eyebrow cull (51 sites). Removing ~40 micro-labels is an editorial pass per view; doing it blind would flatten screens that currently rely on them.
  • Dark-mode token migration (46 hexes). Most of what remains is palette data (avatar pastels, status tints) or hover shades, not surface decisions. The .dark .bg-white shim still does real work; retiring it means touching every call site that pins a literal white, one visual check at a time.
  • Remaining arbitrary type sizes (~60 × text-[13px] / [15px]). These already sit on ladder values (13 = meta, 15 = ui). Converting them is a find-and-replace that only pays off once a lint guard exists to stop the next one appearing.

If the brand blue must remain literally #1591dc on the button, the only alternative is raising button labels to 19px+, which costs far more layout than the four points of lightness.

What to protect

A review that only lists faults is a bad review. Several things here are better than what most enterprise systems ship, and the optimizations above must not sand them off.

Status never rides on hue alone. The five-glyph system (check · clock · x · (i) · minus) is drawn as clean Lucide-geometry strokes, masked to currentColor, and it agrees across pills, dots and badges. That is the single most valuable pattern in the codebase — a colour-blind evaluator can read the queue. Extend it, never replace it.
States are designed, not defaulted. Skeletons predict the loaded layout (SkeletonRows, SkeletonKanban), errors are retryable cards, empties are illustrated states with a next action, and the mobile shell has real pull-to-refresh with a session re-fetch. Most products at this stage have a spinner and a blank div.
The keyboard story is real. A global :focus-visible ring that respects each element's own silhouette (no radius snapping), ⌘K available on tablet and desktop, 156 aria-labels, and a command palette reachable from mobile without a tab.
Restraint. One typeface, one brand hue, two shadow voices, no gradients on surfaces, no icon soup. The comments in globals.css read like a design spec written by someone who had to defend it — that discipline is why the four P0/P1 fixes are enough.

And 37 of 42 animated files honour prefers-reduced-motion. Close the last five and the app is genuinely accessible to motion-sensitive users — a bar most government systems do not clear.
